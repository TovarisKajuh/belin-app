# The GTM mentor buildout: how the repo learns to launch Belin

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax. Phase 5 requires superpowers:writing-skills.

**Goal:** Turn "how do I launch Belin" from a question that gets improvised answers into a discipline the repo carries: a knowledge base with honest epistemics, distilled into operational skills, wired into CLAUDE.md, producing the documents, scripts, calendars, outreach plans, call frameworks and event plans a solo non-developer founder can execute in Slovenia first and DACH second.

**This plan executes nothing.** It is the full specification of the buildout; work starts when the founder says go.

**v2, 2026-08-19, after founder review of v1.** What changed: the source-everything rule is replaced by the three-tier epistemic system in Part I, because experts run on calibrated judgment, not footnotes; the outreach-law material is demoted from a gate to one input inside the channels research, kept only because it decides channel order in Germany; and every part of the plan is specified to the level of contents, not just file names.

---

## Part I: The mentor's operating model

### I.1 Three kinds of knowledge, three different rules

The v1 rule ("every claim carries a source") was wrong, founder-corrected, and the correction is itself an insight worth encoding. A mentor who can only speak in citations is a librarian. The base distinguishes:

**FACT.** Verifiable, and being wrong is expensive: prices, tool capabilities, dates, legal constraints, market counts, competitor features. Rule: verified before asserted, source and date recorded. This tier exists because of the FocuSee episode, and it is the only tier where the old rule survives.

**PATTERN.** Synthesized regularities: "document carousels outperform image posts on LinkedIn", "construction owners buy on referral and at fairs, not from ads", "pilots without an end date never convert". Rule: name the inputs the pattern was distilled from (essays, case studies, interviews, the founder's own experience) once, then use it freely. Patterns are the mentor's working vocabulary.

**JUDGMENT.** The instinct tier, explicitly legitimate: "open the demo on the phone, not the dashboard", "never discount the rate, extend the pilot instead", "post the defect story before the milestone story". Rule: marked J on first use, never blocked for lacking a source, and every judgment that becomes advice gets an outcome recorded in the field log. Judgment earns rank by being right, loses it by being wrong, and the file says which has happened. That is what calibrated instinct means, and it is the only honest version of "trust me" available to a mentor made of text.

### I.2 The field log

`docs/gtm/field-log.md`, append-only. Every outreach batch, every call, every demo, every post gets a line: date, what was tried, which knowledge it stood on, what happened. This is the mechanism that turns the base from a library into experience. Patterns and judgments cite the field log back once reality has voted.

### I.3 The mentoring cadence

- **Monday review** (gtm-weekly-review skill): scorecard, pipeline moves, next week's touches planned to the name, one knowledge gap picked and filled.
- **Before every demo** (gtm-demo-call skill): prospect prep sheet: who they are, their projects (public sources plus the founder's knowledge), predicted objections, the one screen most likely to land.
- **After every demo**: ten-minute debrief into the field log while it is fresh: what they asked, where they leaned in, the exact words of any objection.
- **Monthly**: strategy pass: what the numbers say, what gets more effort, what gets cut. The mentor argues from the base; the founder decides.

### I.4 Decision rights and the sycophancy rule

The mentor proposes with a recommendation and its reasoning, the founder disposes. Overruled recommendations are logged with both positions, not relitigated. And the standing anti-sycophancy rule: the mentor's job includes saying "that post is weak", "this prospect is stalling you", "you skipped follow-ups two weeks running". A mentor that only encourages is a liability wearing a friendly face.

## Part II: The curriculum

Each knowledge file below lists the questions it must answer and the candidate sources to mine (sources are leads until checked during execution). A file is done when its questions have answers with the right tier labels.

### II.1 `founder-groundtruth.md` (the intake, Part VII)
The founder is primary source number one. Everything else calibrates against this file.

### II.2 `market-icp-si.md`
Must answer: how many companies in Slovenia install or contract solar at a scale that can pay; segmented how (resi-only installers, commercial EPCs, electro companies with a solar arm, energy companies); the named top 30 with size signals; which of them subcontract montage and to whom (those are the two-sided wins); where they gather; who in the founder's network touches which. Candidate sources: AJPES and bizi.si registries by dejavnost code, Borzen and Agencija za energijo connection statistics, GZS member lists, the founder.

### II.3 `market-icp-dach.md`
Same questions at lower resolution for DE and AT, plus: the German pilot contact's segment profiled first; how German EPC-to-sub relationships differ (VOB culture, Nachunternehmer norms); minimum company size where the pain is acute.

### II.4 `buyer-psychology-construction.md`
Must answer: how construction SME owners actually adopt tools (who saw it where, who vouched for it); the role of the wife-or-accountant back office in the decision; why subscriptions get resisted and per-project costs do not; what killed tools they tried before; the seasonal rhythm of attention (winter is planning season, summer is no-time season). Candidate sources: Procore S-1 GTM sections, ServiceTitan S-1, PlanRadar and Craftnote founder interviews, vertical SaaS essays, the founder.

### II.5 `competitor-gtm-history.md`
Must answer: what PlanRadar, Craftnote, Capmo and 123erfasst each did in their first two years (channel, first customers, pricing moves); what Procore and ServiceTitan prove about vertical depth beating horizontal breadth; what every one of them still does badly that Belin's positioning can name.

### II.6 `channels-linkedin.md`
Must answer: current reach mechanics by format (document, image, video, text) with the van der Blom report cross-checked against one other study; dwell time and early-engagement mechanics; personal profile vs company page for a founder; Sales Navigator: real cost, what a saved search can express, whether this ICP is even findable in it (Slovenian solar companies are small and sparsely labeled, a FACT to verify before paying); connect-note conventions that do not read as spam in SI and DACH business culture.

### II.7 `channels-referrals.md` (new in v2, and probably channel number one)
In a market of a few hundred buyers, warm paths beat every cold channel. Must answer: mapping the founder's first and second degree network onto the ICP list; the ask script for an intro (give the introducer the exact two sentences to forward); how the free sub side becomes a referral engine (every sub on a pilot project touches other EPCs weekly, THE structural growth loop of the two-sided model); when and how to ask a pilot customer for one named intro.

### II.8 `channels-cold-calling.md`
Must answer: what a first call to an SI solar EPC sounds like in the trade's own register; gatekeeper handling in five-person companies (often none, the owner answers); best call windows around site hours (J to start: early morning and Friday afternoon); the legal position per country in one paragraph each, researched from primary texts, because it decides whether the German motion may include cold calls at all. That single paragraph is the whole surviving remnant of v1's law gate.

### II.9 `channels-events.md`
Must answer: the real list with dates and costs: Intersolar and The smarter E Munich, Solar Solutions Düsseldorf, MOS Celje, SI energy days and chamber events; walk-the-floor vs exhibit math at Belin's stage (J: walking with a phone full of product beats a booth by an order of magnitude per euro); how to book meetings before a fair instead of hoping at it; the one-screen fair demo (which single screen, rehearsed to 60 seconds).

### II.10 `channels-media-associations.md`
Must answer: which trade media take founder contributions (pv magazine DE, Montel Energetika SI, installer association newsletters); what formats they accept; whether GZS and BSW-Solar have vendor-visible programs; realistic timelines from pitch to print.

### II.11 `craft-founder-led-sales.md`
Distills: Founding Sales (Kazanjy) on outbound as a founder, first sales materials, and early pipeline discipline; The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick) on discovery that does not fish for compliments; the parts of both that survive contact with a founder who has single-digit weekly selling hours. Output includes the weekly operating rhythm at three hour-budgets (4, 8, 15 hours).

### II.12 `craft-positioning.md`
Works Dunford's method FOR Belin, on paper: competitive alternative is WhatsApp plus Excel plus a folder of PDFs, not PlanRadar; unique attributes are the money loop (countdown, deemed approval, signed acceptance, auto-invoice) and documents-as-evidence; the category decision made deliberately (Bautagebuch app vs collaboration platform vs "the tool that closes solar projects"), with the losing options and why recorded.

### II.13 `craft-offer-and-pilot.md`
Must answer: pilot design that converts (one project, defined success criteria written at the start, an end date, a named next step at signup); the founder-pricing offer as already modeled (3 EUR per kWp locked 12 months, floor and cap unchanged); guarantee design (J: "free until it saves you time" is strong at zero customers, dangerous at ten, the file must say when to retire it); why never to discount the rate and what to concede instead (cap, ceiling, duration).

### II.14 `craft-demo-and-close.md`
Must answer: discovery questions in the buyer's words (five, not twenty); the 25 minute demo arc mapped to real Belin screens, opening on the crew phone (J) because the wow is 30-second reporting, then live dashboard, countdown, signed zapisnik, invoice; the close as a scheduling act, not a persuasion act; the ten objections with honest answers, including "what if you disappear" (a solo founder WILL be asked, and the honest answer involves EU-hosted data, PDF export, and the customer keeping every document regardless).

## Part III: The assets, specified

Everything in `docs/gtm/assets/` is produced FROM Part II files and points back at them.

- **icp.md**: three tiers. Tier A: SI commercial EPCs, 5 to 50 people, subcontracting montage (the two-sided win, both sides of the product light up). Tier B: SI installers doing their own montage (one-sided value now, tab-bar side only). Tier C: DACH, activated only when the DE gate items (Impressum, privacy, deliverability, German assets) are green. Per-prospect fields: name, tier, kWp per year estimate, sub relationships, warm path, state, next touch date. Disqualifiers written down (resi-only one-man bands: users, not buyers).
- **90-day-plan.md**: three blocks. Days 1 to 30, Foundation: funnel fixes (the login dead end), carousel brochure, first three posts, first ten Tier A conversations, all warm. Days 31 to 60, Motion: posting rhythm standing, 25 touches per week, first demos, first pilot signed. Days 61 to 90, Compounding: pilot evidence becomes content, referral asks begin, DE gate items done, German pilot contact activated. Every week has named deliverables and a kill-or-continue question.
- **posting-calendar.md**: eight weeks, two posts per week, drafted, each tagged with an archetype from the researched set (candidate archetypes to validate in II.6: problem story, artifact showcase, build-in-public, contrarian take, number post, before-and-after, question post, milestone with proof). Every post built from a groundtruth story or a real product artifact, never from generic founder content.
- **outreach-playbook.md**: sequences per tier and country. Tier A default (J, to calibrate): warm intro ask first; where no path exists, LinkedIn connect with a one-line note, then a genuine comment on their content, then the DM with one specific observation about THEIR projects and one question, then the call. Spacing in working days, exit criteria after three touches without signal, and the rule that every sequence ends in a proposed call, not "thoughts?".
- **call-script-si.md / call-script-de.md**: skeleton, not teleprompter: 20 second opener in trade register, one pain probe, the 30 second story (a real one from groundtruth), the single ask (25 minutes with their real project on screen). Objection branches inline.
- **demo-script.md**: the II.14 arc as a checklist with the exact demo project state to pre-stage (the seed already provides it), including the recovery moves when wifi dies or a screen breaks (show the PDF folder instead: the artifact always works).
- **pilot-offer.md**: one page, Slovenian: what the pilot includes, the founder price and its 12 month lock, the success criteria template to fill in together at signature, the end date, what happens after (convert at list, or part as friends with all their PDFs).
- **scorecard.md**: leading metrics (touches, conversations, demos booked) weekly; lagging (pilots, conversions) monthly. Initial targets marked J and honest for a solo founder: on the order of 25 touches, 8 conversations, 2 demos per week at the 8 hour budget, 1 pilot per month in SI. Calibrated after four weeks of field log, not defended against it.
- **pipeline.md**: states: identified, touched, in conversation, demo held, pilot offered, pilot live, paying, parked, lost with reason. Hygiene rules: every live prospect has a next touch date or it is parked; loss reasons are verbatim quotes, not paraphrases.

## Part IV: The skills, specified

Each is a `.claude/skills/<name>/SKILL.md`, written with superpowers:writing-skills, passing the cold-session test: correct output from the skill alone, no conversational context.

1. **gtm-linkedin-post**: trigger: any post drafting or review. Inputs: topic or draft, the calendar, groundtruth stories. Procedure: pick archetype, apply the researched format rules, hook in the first two lines, no link in body, first-comment link, one ask. Output: the post plus posting time plus the engagement plan for the first hour.
2. **gtm-outreach**: trigger: "who do I contact and what do I say". Inputs: pipeline.md, icp.md, playbook. Procedure: pick the week's names by tier and state, generate each touch personalized from real signals, schedule follow-ups. Output: the touch list with texts, ready to send, plus pipeline updates.
3. **gtm-demo-call**: trigger: a demo is booked or just ended. Prep mode outputs the prospect sheet and stages the demo state; debrief mode interviews the founder for ten minutes and writes the field log entry and next step.
4. **gtm-pilot-close**: trigger: a demo went well. Procedure: fill the pilot offer template with their names and their project, define success criteria wording, set the end date, draft the follow-up message that carries it. Output: the signable one-pager and the send text.
5. **gtm-weekly-review**: trigger: Monday or "weekly review". Procedure: read scorecard and field log, compute the week's numbers against targets, surface the uncomfortable line (the sycophancy rule lives here), plan next week's touches, pick the knowledge gap. Output: the week's one-page brief.

## Part V: Execution phases

Calibrated against this repo's delivered history: five focused sessions.

- [ ] **Session 1: scaffold plus intake plus market.** Tree, INDEX.md, CLAUDE.md section. Founder intake (Part VII) recorded into groundtruth. II.2 and II.3 researched, the named SI top 30 drafted. Gate: founder confirms the top 30 smells right; if not, the registries were read wrong and the file reworks before anything downstream builds on it.
- [ ] **Session 2: buyers, competitors, channels.** II.4, II.5, II.6, II.7, II.8, II.9, II.10. Gate: the channel order per country is written and argued.
- [ ] **Session 3: craft plus positioning.** II.11 to II.14. Gate: the positioning file's category decision, founder-approved, because every asset inherits its language.
- [ ] **Session 4: assets.** All of Part III, each pointing at its knowledge. Gate: the founder reads the 90-day plan and the first two weeks of the calendar and would actually do them.
- [ ] **Session 5: skills plus wiring.** Part IV, CLAUDE.md routing, memory update, INDEX.md complete. Gate: the definition of done, tested cold.

## Part VI: The risk register

Failure modes of solo-founder launches, each with its countermeasure built into the system above:

1. **Content stops in week three.** Countermeasure: eight weeks pre-drafted; the weekly review re-drafts, never starts from blank.
2. **Pipeline becomes a diary of hope.** Countermeasure: next-touch-date-or-parked rule; the review counts touches, not feelings.
3. **Demos that end in "sounds great" and nothing.** Countermeasure: the close is a scheduling act; a demo without a booked next step is logged as a miss, by rule.
4. **Pilots drift forever.** Countermeasure: end date and success criteria at signature, in the template, non-optional.
5. **Price capitulation in the room.** Countermeasure: the concession ladder is decided in advance (cap, ceiling, duration, never the rate) and lives in the close skill.
6. **The German motion starts too early.** Countermeasure: Tier C is gated on the named DE items; the plan says no DE outreach until green, and the review enforces it.
7. **Founder time collapses to zero in a build-heavy week.** Countermeasure: the 4-hour-budget rhythm exists in II.11; the weekly review shrinks the plan instead of silently dropping it.
8. **Knowledge base rot.** Countermeasure: staleness dates in INDEX.md; the review picks one gap weekly.
9. **Mentor sycophancy.** Countermeasure: the rule in I.4, embedded in the review skill's procedure, with the explicit permission this plan grants it in writing: say the uncomfortable line.
10. **Winning likes instead of pilots.** Countermeasure: the scorecard has no reach metrics on the front page; DMs and conversations are the counted fruit of content.

## Part VII: The intake (session 1, recorded into groundtruth)

Stories: the WhatsApp search that cost hours; the worst Regiestunden dispute you have seen; an acceptance that went wrong; the moment you decided to build this. People: which EPCs know you by name; which subs; who owes you a favor; who is the German contact and what exactly was said last. You: hours per week for selling, honestly, and which days; will you cold call in Slovenian; will you call in German or is DE writing-first; what does your current LinkedIn audience consist of; what have you already posted or promised anyone. Money: monthly tool budget; one event budget; how long the runway patience is. Red lines: anything you refuse to do (some founders will not post personal stories; the calendar must know).

## Definition of done

A fresh session, given only this repo, can: draft an outreach sequence for a named Austrian EPC that respects the researched channel order; produce next week's two posts in the founder's voice from his real stories; prep him for tomorrow's demo with the prospect sheet and the staged demo state; move a warm prospect to a signed pilot with the one-pager; and run the Monday review including the uncomfortable line. Each move cites its file, or says honestly that the base has no answer yet, and every judgment it leans on carries its current field-log record.
