## 1. What the repo already knows (no need to answer)

**FACT, from HANDOFF.md.** Jan co-runs **AVESOL**, a solar installation subcontractor working for EPCs in Germany, Austria and Slovenia. Non-developer. Deep firsthand knowledge of the EPC-to-subcontractor process, from the subcontractor's side.

**This is the most underused asset in the entire go-to-market, and it deserves to be stated plainly:**

1. **He is the customer's counterparty.** He has stood on the sub side of exactly the relationship Belin manages. Every pain in the product is one he has personally been on the receiving end of. In a discovery call he is not a vendor asking what hurts, he is a peer describing what hurts and being recognised.
2. **He already has EPC relationships.** AVESOL works for EPCs today. Those are not cold prospects, they are companies that already pay him, trust his work and take his calls. **J: the first ten conversations should be with EPCs AVESOL already invoices.** No cold channel can compete with that.
3. **He has the sub side pre-seeded.** AVESOL itself is the free subcontractor on any pilot, which means every pilot starts with both sides staffed by someone who wants it to work.
4. **He has a credible reason to have built it**, which is the single hardest thing for a founder to fake: "I built this because I was the one losing the arguments."

**PATTERN:** vertical SaaS founders who came from the trade close early customers at rates that generic founders cannot match, because the first meeting skips the credibility phase entirely. His accent, his references and his war stories are the moat in the first twenty conversations.

## 2. Stories, TO ANSWER

The posts, the call openers and the demo all need real stories. Invented ones are recognised instantly by people in the trade. Needed:

1. **The paperwork search.** The worst time you hunted for evidence after the fact. What were you looking for, where was it, how long did it take, what did it cost.
2. **The Regiestunden dispute.** The worst argument about hours you have been in or watched. What was claimed, what was paid, what evidence existed or did not.
3. **The acceptance that went wrong.** A prevzem or Abnahme with defects, disputes, or missing signatures. What happened afterwards.
4. **The decision moment.** What specifically made you start building instead of continuing to complain.
5. **The near-miss.** Any time AVESOL nearly lost money and evidence saved it, or did lose money because evidence did not exist.

For each: roughly when, which country, the amounts if you remember them, and whether the other party can be named or must be anonymised.

## 3. Network, TO ANSWER

1. **EPCs AVESOL currently works for.** Names, country, rough size, your contact there and their role, and how the relationship stands.
2. **EPCs AVESOL has worked for before**, and why it ended.
3. **Other subcontractors you know**, since they are the free side and the referral engine.
4. **The German pilot contact.** Who exactly, what company, what was agreed, when you last spoke, what state it is in now.
5. **Anyone who owes you a favour**, or who would make an introduction if asked.
6. **Your LinkedIn audience.** Roughly how many connections, and what they consist of: installers, EPCs, suppliers, or people unrelated to solar.

## 4. Capacity, TO ANSWER

1. **Hours per week for selling**, honestly, and which days or times. The whole plan is sized against this number, and an inflated answer produces a plan that collapses in week three.
2. **Will you cold call in Slovenian?** Yes or no changes the entire Slovenian channel order.
3. **Will you call in German?** If no, the German motion is writing-first and the pilot contact carries it alone.
4. **Are you comfortable posting personal stories under your own name?** Some founders are not, and the posting calendar must know before it is written.
5. **Monthly tool budget** (Sales Navigator is about 100 EUR per month) and **one-event budget** (a trade fair visit, travel included).

## 5. Red lines, TO ANSWER

Anything you refuse to do. Cold calls, personal posts, video with your face, giving discounts, naming AVESOL clients publicly. Write them down now so no asset is built on something you will not execute.

## 6. Open tension to resolve

**The AVESOL conflict of interest.** You sell Belin to EPCs while running a subcontractor that competes for their work. This will come up, probably in the first ten conversations, and it needs an answer you have decided in advance rather than improvised in a meeting.

**J, a starting position to accept or reject:** name it first, before they do. "I run a montage company, which is why I know exactly what goes wrong between EPCs and their subs. Belin does not show me your projects, and your subs are not my leads." Then have the technical facts ready about data separation, because a sharp buyer will ask and a vague answer there costs the deal. Whether that claim is currently true of the database is a question for the product side, and it needs to be true before the sentence is spoken.
