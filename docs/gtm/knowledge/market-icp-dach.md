# Market and ICP: Germany and Austria

**Last verified:** 2026-08-19
**Confidence:** high on national figures, medium on structure, low on named targets (none identified yet beyond the founder's own contact)
**Refresh trigger:** quarterly, when Bundesnetzagentur and PV Austria publish; or when the German pilot contact changes state

## 1. Germany in numbers

**FACT.** 2025 additions were **16.6 GW**, against 17.7 GWp in 2024, a mild decline. Q1 2026 came in at about **3.5 GWp, down 6 percent** year on year. Germany has now missed its own 20 GW per year target two years running.

**FACT, and the most operationally useful German statistic found:** the share of electrical trade businesses that install PV at all fell from **57.1 percent in 2024 to 52.1 percent in 2025** (ZVEH spring survey). Installations by the electrical trades fell from 395,000 to 355,000, and building-mounted PV specifically from about 360,000 to 325,000 units.

**Read that correctly.** Germany's megawatts are roughly flat while the number of installing businesses is falling. That means **the work is consolidating into fewer, larger companies**. Fewer, bigger firms doing more projects each is precisely the shape of company that needs subcontractor coordination, and precisely the shape that can afford software. **J:** this is a structurally favourable trend for Belin even though the headline reads as decline.

## 2. Austria in numbers

**FACT.** 2025 additions fell to **1,634 MW, down 22 percent** on 2024, the lowest in three years. Q3 2025 alone was 305 MW, a three-year quarterly low. PV Austria's managing director Vera Immitzer has publicly called the pressure on the industry immense. Austria's own ÖNIP target is 2,000 MW per year, now out of reach.

**FACT.** PV Austria states the decline **primarily affects private rooftop systems up to 20 kW**.

## 3. The pattern across all three countries

| Market | 2025 added | Change | Where the pain is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slovenia | 146.5 MW | -51 % | Residential collapsed 77 %, C&I only -8 % |
| Austria | 1,634 MW | -22 % | Explicitly the sub-20 kW residential segment |
| Germany | 16.6 GW | -6 % | Installer count falling faster than volume |

**PATTERN, now visible in three independent markets: the residential solar boom is over and the commercial segment is holding.** Every one of these markets is telling the same story from a different angle, and it is the story Belin was built for without knowing it.

**J, the strategic consequence:** the tailwind is not "solar is growing". It is "the surviving work is commercial, commercial work is subcontracted, and companies arriving in that segment from residential have no process for subcontracted work". Belin sells to the migration, not to the boom.

## 4. Why Germany still comes second

Not for market reasons, the German market is by far the largest of the three. For readiness reasons, and they are concrete and this repo already knows them:

1. **No Impressum and no privacy policy.** Legally expected of a commercial German-facing site. Deferred by the founder, but it gates German outreach at scale rather than a single warm pilot.
2. **Mail deliverability unverified.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the sending domain are unchecked, and German corporate spam filters are the front door.
3. **German-language assets do not exist yet.** The UI is translated; the sales assets, posts and call scripts are not.
4. **No German reference customer.** The single most valuable German asset would be the pilot contact converted and quotable.

**J:** the correct German motion for now is exactly one account, the founder's existing contact, run to a successful pilot and turned into a case study. One German reference is worth more than fifty cold German touches, and it costs a fraction of the effort.

## 5. What is different about the German buyer

**PATTERN, to be deepened in buyer-psychology-construction.md:** German and Austrian construction runs on VOB/B norms that Belin already implements (the six working day Regiestunden clock, deemed approval, acceptance with reservations, retention). This is not a coincidence, the product was built for it, and it is the sharpest differentiator against generic tools when talking to a German EPC. In Slovenia the same mechanics are useful but less codified, so the language must change per country: in Germany name the paragraph, in Slovenia name the argument it prevents.
