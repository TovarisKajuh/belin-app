## 1. The market in numbers

**FACT.** Slovenia added **146.5 MW** of solar in 2025, against 298.8 MW in 2024. That is a **51 percent collapse in one year**. Cumulative capacity is about 1.65 GW across roughly 70,000 grid-connected units.

**FACT.** The 2025 split, from the Slovenian Photovoltaic Association via pv magazine:

| Segment | 2025 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial and industrial (C&I) | 92.6 MW | 100.8 MW | **-8 %** |
| Utility scale (3 systems in 2025) | 9.4 MW | 6.5 MW | +45 % |
| Residential (derived: total minus the above) | **~44.5 MW** | **~191.5 MW** | **-77 %** |

## 2. The finding that should shape everything

**The Slovenian solar collapse is almost entirely residential. The commercial segment barely moved.**

Residential fell by roughly three quarters. C&I fell by eight percent. And **C&I is exactly Belin's segment**: commercial roofs, multi-day jobs, montage subcontracted to crews, acceptance protocols, hour sheets, retention.

Three consequences, all **J**, all to be tested against real conversations and logged:

1. **Belin's market is the resilient one.** A prospect reading "the solar market is dying" in the trade press is describing a market Belin does not serve.
2. **Companies are migrating INTO Belin's segment.** A firm that lived on 10 kWp house roofs in 2024 is chasing commercial roofs in 2026 because that is where the volume went. Those firms are newly dealing with subcontracted crews, VOB-style acceptance and Regiestunden disputes for the first time, and they have no process for any of it. **That is the single best trigger event Belin has**, and it is invisible unless you know the segment split.
3. **The pitch must be about protecting money, not saving time.** In a contracting market with squeezed margins, "digitalization" and "efficiency" are luxury words. "You will not lose the argument about those 19 hours" is a survival word. Time-saving is the second argument, never the first.

**PATTERN.** A contracting market makes buyers slower and more price-sensitive, but it makes the pain of a lost dispute proportionally larger, because there is less margin to absorb it. Sell the downside, not the upside.

## 3. Market structure and who the buyer is

**FACT.** Slovenia has no published count of solar installation companies. ZSFV (Združenje slovenske fotovoltaike), the industry association, states "more than 20 members", and its member list contains manufacturers and traders as well as installers, so it is not an installer census.

**J, to be replaced by AJPES work:** the number of Slovenian companies that both (a) contract commercial solar and (b) subcontract montage is likely in the **low hundreds at most, and the genuinely addressable Tier A set is probably 30 to 60 companies**. This is a market where the entire buyer universe fits in a spreadsheet and can be worked by name. That single fact should govern the whole go-to-market: precision over volume, because there is no volume to be had and every clumsy first touch permanently removes a name.

## 4. The tiers

**Tier A, the two-sided win.** Slovenian companies contracting commercial solar (roughly 50 kWp and up) that subcontract montage to crews. Both sides of Belin light up: the EPC gets evidence and control, the sub gets a free tool and a compliance passport. Size signal: 5 to 50 people. These are the first calls.

**Tier B, one-sided.** Companies doing their own montage with in-house crews. They get the crew reporting, the diary, the documents and the money loop; they do not get the two-sided value. Still a real customer, but a weaker story and no referral engine into subs.

**Tier C, DACH.** Gated on the German readiness items (Impressum and privacy pages, mail deliverability, German assets). See market-icp-dach.md.

**Disqualifiers, written down so time is not spent on them:** one-man residential installers (they are users of the free side at best, never buyers); pure EPC developers who never touch installation; equipment distributors; anyone whose annual volume is under about 200 kWp, because at that size the paperwork pain is real but the budget is not.
